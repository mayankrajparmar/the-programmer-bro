We teach how AI works, and we use AI tools to help us do it. Here's exactly where, and how we keep the quality up.

## AI news

Our news stories are drafted with the help of AI:

1. A program collects new stories from the sources we follow, such as AI labs' blogs, tech news sites and Indian news outlets.
2. An AI model, Google's Gemini, writes a short headline and a two- or three-sentence summary of each story, using only the source's headline and its short description.
3. **A person reads every draft**, edits it where needed and decides whether to publish it. Nothing is published automatically.

Every story links to its original source. Summaries are short and can miss detail, so read the source before relying on a story.

## Lessons and quizzes

We may use AI tools to help research, draft and edit lessons and quizzes, in the same way a writer might use a spell checker or a research assistant. **A person checks every lesson against the sources listed at its end before it's published**, and the date it was last reviewed is shown on the lesson.

## What AI doesn't do here

- It doesn't publish anything on its own.
- It doesn't decide what's true: our sources and the person checking them do.
- It never sees anything about you. We don't collect personal data (see our [privacy policy](/privacy)).

## Found a mistake?

AI tools and people both get things wrong sometimes. If something looks off, [tell us](/contact) which page and what's wrong, and we'll check it and fix it.
